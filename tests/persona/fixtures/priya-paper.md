# On the Equivalence of Mass and Energy

## Abstract

We revisit the classical result $E = mc^2$ in light of recent observational data from neutrino oscillation experiments[^1]. The relation, while well-established, continues to admit refinement at the boundary where quantum field corrections become non-negligible.

## Introduction

The mass-energy relation, written in its compact form

$$E = mc^2,$$

is among the most widely-cited results in physics, yet it is also among the most frequently misunderstood[^2]. In this paper we examine three common misreadings and propose a pedagogical framework that avoids them.

## Method

We follow the convention of natural units $\hbar = c = 1$ throughout, restoring factors of $c$ only in final expressions intended for experimental comparison.

The Lagrangian density we adopt is

$$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu \phi)(\partial^\mu \phi) - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2,$$

a free scalar field with mass $m$.

## Discussion

A first observation is that the equivalence is not, properly, a statement about mass and energy as separate quantities, but about a single invariant quantity expressed in two different unit systems.
