# The Long Walk Home

There is a particular quality to the light in late October that I have never been able to capture in a photograph, and so I write about it instead. It pours sideways through the bare birches, low and amber, the way honey might pour from a jar tipped just so, slow enough that you could draw the line with a pencil if you were quick.

## Morning

I left the house at seven. The frost was still on the grass and the dog had refused, again, to come out from under the bed. I walked anyway. There is a kind of writing one can only do on foot, and a kind of thinking that requires the small punctuations of breath and step.

> "Walking is also an ambulation of mind."
> — Gretel Ehrlich

## What I noticed

- The smell of woodsmoke from the Petersens' chimney, two streets over.
- A single crow on the post by the mailbox, watching me the way crows watch — not interested, exactly, but aware.
- The feeling, around mile two, that the essay I had been struggling with for three weeks had a shape after all, and the shape was simpler than I had been making it.

## Afternoon

By the time I came back, the light had moved. It was no longer the morning's slow honey but something thinner, more silver, a light that announces winter the way a single struck note announces a song. I sat at the kitchen table with my notebook and wrote for an hour without looking up.

The essay, when I read it back, was not what I had set out to write. It was better.

## A small confession

I had thought, when I started, that I knew what I was doing. I no longer think that, and I find I am not sorry.
